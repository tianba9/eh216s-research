The Economics of One Aircraft

This chapter replaces macro narrative with the accounts of one aircraft. Three market calibers are shown separately; order structure matters more than order count.

01Market Size: Three Calibers, Not Interchangeable

CCID reports RMB 715.1bn for 2025 in a narrow definition and RMB 859.2bn in a broader one; CAAC projects RMB 1.5tn. The boundaries differ, so the figures cannot be added or substituted.

02Demand: Order Structure Matters More Than Count

Buyers are local state-owned platforms and lessors; most indicative orders carry no deposit. Several cities have paused direct subsidies, shifting policy from firm support to infrastructure.

03Capacity: The Daily Sortie Ceiling

A 25-minute flight plus 120 minutes of charging and 10 minutes of turnaround gives a 155-minute cycle, or three sorties per eight-hour day and 900 per year.

04Unit Economics

Fixed annual cost of RMB 972,000 plus electricity gives about RMB 1,112 per sortie and RMB 556 per seat, close to the published RMB 300–500 ticket range.

05Three Scenarios & Sensitivity

At 120 minutes of recharge the model barely breaks even. At 60 minutes, cost per seat falls to RMB 340. At five-minute battery swapping, it reaches RMB 139 and the operation becomes profitable.

06Chapter Summary

The lever is not battery energy but recharge time. Today the business sells aircraft, not transport; 62.0% gross margin comes from hardware, not operations.
