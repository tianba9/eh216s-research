The Numbers Hold Up

Three independent checks test whether the published figures are credible: time closure, order of magnitude and benchmarking.

01Three Verification Methods

Time closure asks whether range, endurance and cruise speed come from one mission profile. Magnitude checks test power and energy against physical limits. Benchmarking looks for abnormal outliers among peers.

02Time-Closure Check

A 30 km trip at 90 km/h takes 20 minutes; adding 2 minutes of climb and 3 of descent gives 25, exactly the published endurance. The three figures fit one flight profile rather than being independent marketing numbers.

03Benchmarking

Against Joby S4 and Archer Midnight, EH216-S trails by an order of magnitude in speed and range. Its lead is certification progress, not performance. Judging it by range alone produces the wrong conclusion.

04Maturity Assessment

Structure, propulsion and production are at TRL 9; flight control and energy at 8; autonomy and airspace integration lower. What is mature is the aircraft; what is immature is the operating system.

05Where the Bottleneck Really Is

Three layers: battery specific energy, the absence of wing-borne lift, and a 120-minute recharge against 25 minutes of flight. The third is structural and hardest to fix by software.

06If Verification Were Possible: A Flight-Test Outline

Five tests would settle the estimates: weigh a battery pack, measure sortie energy, record the charging window, log 30 days of daily sorties, and inject a single motor failure on a test rig.

07Chapter Summary

All three checks pass, so the figures are credible. Passing does not mean leading: EH216-S is a year or two behind in performance and years ahead in certification. The hardest bottleneck is on the ground, not in the air.
