Flying Is Not Operating

The same aircraft is a product above a scenic site, a risk above a city and a public resource on an emergency corridor. Scenarios are not chosen by marketing; they are the result of four conditions acting together: demand rigidity, ability to pay, regulatory permission and capacity matching.

01Scenario Matrix

Two dimensions are used for screening: the horizontal axis is the availability of regulation and airspace, and the vertical axis is demand rigidity and willingness to pay. Only scenarios in the upper-right quadrant meet the conditions for deployment at the current stage.

02Five-Dimension Scoring

To avoid the vague judgment that "everything looks good", each scenario is broken into five scoreable requirements, each rated from 1 to 5.

Table 4-1　Five-dimension scoring of five scenario classes (5-point scale; higher is more favourable)
ScenarioDemand rigidityAbility to payRegulatory feasibilityCapacity matchingTotal
Low-altitude sightseeing345517
Island / mountain shuttle444416
Emergency medical transfer532414
Aerial advertising / shows233513
Urban air commuting451111

Scoring basis: demand rigidity considers whether irreplaceable time value exists; ability to pay considers end-customer ticket price; regulatory feasibility considers whether airspace and vertiports are open; capacity matching considers whether daily sorties per aircraft can meet the passenger density of the scenario.

The commuting scenario fails not on demand but on capacity matching. A city's commuting demand is measured in tens of thousands of trips, while one aircraft carries fewer than 10 passengers per day; over a thousand aircraft would need to be airborne simultaneously to form effective supply — untenable across airspace, vertiport and cost dimensions.

03Seven ODD Elements

The operational design domain (ODD) defines "under what conditions flight is allowed". The special condition has limited the operating environment to commercial passenger carrying within segregated low-altitude airspace, which means not all weather, sites or time slots fall within the design scope.

Table 4-2　ODD elements of the EH216-S and current constraints
ElementCurrent statusImpact on operations
AirspaceSegregated low-altitude airspace; flight plans must be filed in advanceCannot mix with manned aircraft; each route requires individual approval
WeatherLimited to visual meteorological conditions; no quantified wind limit publishedHigh winds, thunderstorms and low visibility stop flights; flyable days are limited
VertiportRequires hard, level ground with clearance and crowd separationSite cost and siting difficulty form a hidden threshold
Time slotDaylight operations; no night approval seenOperating window is compressed, capping daily sorties
Population densityPopulation density beneath routes is restrictedDifficult to cross city cores; routes must follow edges or scenic sites
CommunicationsRelies on a dedicated data link with triple redundancyOperations are impossible where link coverage is insufficient
Ground accessVertiports need passenger collection and dispersal facilitiesDetermines total door-to-door time and erodes the speed advantage over ground transport
An underestimated item

Ground access is often overlooked yet directly determines product value. The EH216-S flies 30 km in about 25 minutes, but passengers typically spend more than 30 minutes travelling to and from the vertiport, clearing security and boarding. If the vertiport is not near the origin and destination, total door-to-door time may be worse than driving. The sightseeing scenario works precisely because the vertiport sits inside the scenic site, making access cost zero.

04The Cost of Segregated Airspace

"Segregated airspace" means three things commercially, worth stating separately because it is the most direct link between the current regulatory position and the business model.

Table 4-3　The three commercial meanings of segregated airspace
ImplicationManifestationCommercial consequence
No networkingEach sortie needs a separate filing, making a fixed schedule network difficultCannot offer "go any time" commuting; only pre-booked experience flights are possible
No mixed operationsAirspace shared on a time- and zone-sharing basis with helicopters, drones and manned aircraftLow airspace turnover; mutual obstruction as scale grows
No city accessRoutes avoid densely populated areasHigh-value city-centre scenarios are excluded, lowering the market ceiling

These three are not specific to the EH216-S but common constraints on the whole industry before air traffic management upgrades. They also explain why industry treats the "intelligent low-altitude network" and "fine-grained airspace management" as issues as important as the aircraft itself.

05Deployment Path

The course material judges that "specialised and mass-market scenarios proceed in parallel". Judging by the EH216-S's actual trajectory, the path is closer to three stages, with progressively rising thresholds.

The spans between stages differ. Stage 1 to 2 is an expansion of operational capability; Stage 2 to 3 is a switch of technical paradigm — the latter cannot be crossed through operational optimisation.

06Chapter Verdict

Verdict 1

Scenario screening converges rather than diverges

The five-dimension scores of the five scenario classes decline monotonically from 17 to 11; sightseeing and short shuttle form the only viable combination today. This matches exactly the commercial operations it has actually entered.

Verdict 2

Segregated airspace is the starting point of model design, not a temporary restriction

No networking, no mixed operations and no city access: together these three constraints mean it can only offer pre-booked experiences, not scheduled commuting. Any business plan ignoring this fails.

Verdict 3

Door-to-door time is the hidden killer

A 25-minute flight plus 30 minutes of access and security almost erases the speed advantage over ground transport. The scenario only works if the vertiport is at the destination, which explains why scenic sites come first.

07Chapter Summary

Scenarios are not marketing categories but combinations of demand, regulation and capacity. Five-dimension scoring ranks the five classes from 17 to 11; sightseeing and short shuttle form the only closed-loop combination, matching the commercial operations it has actually entered.

The triple cost of segregated airspace — no networking, no mixed operations, no city access — confines it to pre-booked experiences. Together with door-to-door time almost erasing the speed advantage, commuting is not viable at the current stage.
