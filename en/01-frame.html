Ask the Right Question First, Then Look for the Answer

“Researching a flying vehicle” is a task that cannot be falsified; even ten thousand words cannot tell right from wrong. This chapter splits it into four propositions with explicit criteria and records the search process, so that readers can reproduce the path — or overturn the report's conclusions.

01From Task to Falsifiable Claims

The course assignment requires coverage of three dimensions: technical feasibility, application scenarios and market viability. These three terms are themselves vague and need to be reduced to measurable criteria.

Table 1-1　Four propositions, their criteria and the verdicts of this report
No.PropositionCriterion (under what conditions it is overturned)This report's verdict
P1 The EH216-S's published performance figures are physically self-consistent, with no unexplained exaggeration. Endurance derived from power and energy conservation falls significantly below the claim, or the thrust-to-weight ratio fails. Holds. Energy accounting falls in the 40 kWh range, comparable to a 620 kg-class multirotor; see Chapter 03.
P2 The product's configuration confines its market boundary to short point-to-point trips rather than urban commuting. A commercially operable commuter route exists, and the daily capacity per aircraft can support commuter density. Holds. Theoretical daily sorties per aircraft are about three, an order of magnitude short of commuter capacity density.
P3 The current order volume reflects industrial-policy intent rather than end-user travel demand. Orders come mainly from end consumers or high-frequency operators, with a high deposit ratio. Holds. Customers are mainly local state-owned platforms and leasing companies, and most have not paid a deposit.
P4 Battery specific energy is currently the only technical variable that can change the P2 verdict. A comparable pathway for relieving the constraint exists through configuration, aerodynamics or airspace reform. Partly holds. Airspace and infrastructure are parallel constraints, but until endurance breaks through they do not constitute effective bottleneck relief.
Why designed this way

P1 tests whether the data can be trusted, P2 whether the product positioning is right, P3 whether demand is genuine, and P4 whether the future may change. Together the four cover the technical, scenario and market dimensions, and each can be overturned by a specific set of data — that is research worth writing.

02Search Protocol

Searches followed a priority order of “official first, then standards, then academia, and media last”. The reason is direct: the authoritative sources for product performance are the manufacturer and regulators, not secondary reporting; media's unique value lies in chronology and competitive dynamics, not in parameters.

A practical detail

Searching for “EHang accident” returned mostly irrelevant results, and no accident record confirmed by a regulator was found. This does not mean “no accidents exist”; it only means “no regulator-confirmed accident was found in public channels”. Accordingly this report labels “zero accidents” as a company claim rather than a factual assertion.

04Evidence Grading & Conflict Handling

The most common error in research is not failing to find material but treating secondary sources as first-hand and forecasts as facts. This report uses a two-tier set of rules to control this.

Grading: five labels

Every key figure in the text carries a label, allowing readers to judge how far a statement can be used. Official can be quoted directly; Standard defines requirements; Media analysis needs cross-verification; Company claim is marked as unaudited; Author's estimate must state its assumptions.

Conflict handling: three rules

  1. Conflicts within one dimension: use the official value. The manufacturer's site gives a maximum take-off weight of 620 kg while several secondary sources give 650 kg. This report uses 620 kg and retains a conflict note in the fact sheet — because the manufacturer's site is a first-hand disclosure by the designer, while secondary tables cannot explain their basis for revision.
  2. Different calibers are never merged. Three readings of low-altitude-economy scale are of similar magnitude but different meaning: CCID's narrow-caliber RMB 715.12bn for 2025, CCID Consulting's RMB 859.17bn, and CAAC's broad-caliber RMB 1.5tn. Their statistical boundaries differ; they cannot be added or substituted for one another, and this report presents them separately in Chapter 05.
  3. Forecasts stay labelled as forecasts. The “RMB 2tn by 2030” figure is an institutional projection, not an accomplished fact. When cited, this report always retains its forecast status and issuing body.
A preserved contradiction

There is a discrepancy between the maximum altitude stated on the manufacturer's site and the operating envelope in the special condition (media report 120 m AGL / 1,000 m MSL, while another source gives 200 m AGL / 3,000 m MSL). In the scenarios chapter this report uses the statutory term “low-altitude segregated airspace” rather than a specific altitude figure to support the argument — because that figure is not stated on the official technical page and varies between batches of material.

05Method Limitations

  • No first-hand interviews. This report had no contact with the manufacturer, operator or regulator; all conclusions rest on public material.
  • Key parameters missing. Empty weight, rotor diameter, battery-pack capacity and chemistry are all undisclosed. The power calculation in Chapter 03 therefore relies on assumed ranges and can only serve as an order-of-magnitude judgment, not a design conclusion.
  • Operating data is a company claim. Sortie and safety records cannot be independently verified, so this report uses them at the lowest credibility grade.
  • Market judgments are time-sensitive. Ticketed operation only began in March 2026, and the unit economics model lacks real passenger-flow data; the scenario analysis in Chapter 05 is inference rather than observation.

Acknowledging these limitations does not weaken the conclusions; it defines their scope. This report's position is that when evidence is incomplete, a judgment with boundary conditions is preferable to an assertion without them.

06Chapter Summary

This chapter did not simply search; it made “research” testable. Each of the four propositions is tied to a criterion that data could overturn; searches were graded in the order official, standards, academia, media; and each of the 20 sources is labelled with what it can and cannot support. Conflicts are not smoothed over — 620 kg and 650 kg are placed side by side, and the three market calibers are shown separately.

On method there is only one sentence: the credibility of a conclusion depends on whether a reader can reproduce the same path, not on the length of the argument.
