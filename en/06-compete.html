Certification or Range: Pick One

The passenger eVTOL industry has a real trade-off curve: the more aggressive the configuration, the better the performance and the slower certification. The EH216-S sits at the lower-left end — furthest ahead in certification, weakest in performance. Understanding this curve is necessary to judge how long its lead can last.

01Competitive Landscape

The horizontal axis quantifies certification progress by review stage; the vertical axis is design range. The two are not independent: tilt-rotors and compound wings buy range at the cost of certification complexity.

This figure explains the industry's real competitive logic: no product can occupy the upper-right corner. Joby and Archer trade range for certification time; the EH216-S trades range for certification speed; AeroHT uses a separable design to compress the airworthiness scope to the flying module in exchange for production speed. Each route has its price.

The EH216-S does not lead on total score and is even slightly lower. But this total is misleading — it hides the fact that within the subset "currently operable", the EH216-S is the only option. The other three have not obtained type certificates and, however high their scores, cannot offer service in 2026.

03Does the Moat Hold

Whether the certification lead is a sustainable advantage depends on three conditions. Testing them one by one, the conclusion is "conditionally holds".

Table 6-2　Testing the conditions for the moat
ConditionTest resultJudgment
Low replicability of certificationA new configuration must rebuild its certification basis; the special condition applies only to this model. Joby and Archer follow their own paths and cannot cite EH216-S evidence.Holds. Certification evidence is not transferable.
Operational data can become a barrier9 ×10⁴ sorties of operational data support reliability argumentation and insurance pricing; latecomers must accumulate their own.Partly holds. The data value is real, but the lead converges over time.
The technical route cannot be bypassedIf a compound-wing or tilt-rotor obtains its TC before 2028, its range advantage will directly cover EH216-S scenarios.Does not hold. This is the main threat.

Overall judgment: the moat exists but is about three to four years wide. Its role is not permanent leadership but an entry ticket — "first to market, first to accumulate operational data, first to build customer relationships". The key move during the window is not raising performance but locking in the operating network and customers — consistent with its 2026 strategic focus on advancing regular operations and expanding overseas.

05Overall Judgment

B

Overall feasibility: conditionally feasible

The rating is upper only when three conditions hold together: (1) scenarios locked to sightseeing and short shuttle; (2) recharge time cut below 60 minutes; (3) operating network and customers locked in before 2028. With all three it can be raised to A−; missing any one lowers it to C. This rating is a judgment, not a forecast.

Judgment 1

Technical feasibility: holds, but only within 30 km

Parameters are self-consistent, mass-producible and in commercial operation. Technically feasible as a short-range passenger aircraft; not feasible as an urban commuting tool, and iteration cannot fix this in the near term.

Judgment 2

Scenario feasibility: sightseeing holds, commuting does not

Five-dimension scores of 17 against 11 show a structural gap. Segregated airspace, low capacity and door-to-door time together exclude commuting at the current stage.

Judgment 3

Market feasibility: demand exists, the model is not closed

The baseline break-even fare is about RMB 556 at full occupancy. Reported fares of RMB 299–498 fall below that threshold even with both seats sold.

Judgment 4

Competitive position: a three-year window, outcome depends on actions within it

A three-to-four-year certification lead but performance an order of magnitude behind. The window's value lies in locking in the network and customers, not raising parameters. If a competitor certifies before 2028, the short-shuttle market will be directly covered.

In one sentence

The EH216-S is not a good aircraft but a scarce certificate. It proved something no one had proved before: an unmanned passenger aircraft can pass CAAC certification. The value of that certificate far exceeds its 30 km range.

06Chapter Summary

Certification and performance form a trade-off curve; no product occupies both ends. In the five-dimension comparison the EH216-S does not lead on total score, but within the subset "currently operable" it is the only option.

The moat is about three to four years wide, resting on the non-transferability of certification evidence. The key action in the window is not raising parameters but locking in the network and customers — overall rating B, with three conditions stated in the conclusion.
